# What makes a reel hook, body and ending work

Researched 9 Nov 2026. The rules below are what `src/lib/playbook.ts` feeds the writer and critic.

## How much to trust this

Very little public research measures Reels retention directly, because the platforms don't publish it.
So each source is graded, and the claims are kept to what the source actually supports.

| Grade | Source | What it shows | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strong | [Le Quéré et al., Scientific Reports 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-81575-9), 8,977 A/B tests, pre-registered | Clicks peak at middling concreteness. 50.9% of headlines were too concrete and would lose by adding more detail. Only 8.7% were too vague. | Headlines, not video. A strong analogy, not proof for Reels. |
| Strong | [Loewenstein 1994](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PsychofCuriosity.pdf) | Curiosity is a felt information gap. A medium-sized gap works best. | Theory and lab work. |
| Strong | Berger and Milkman 2012, *Journal of Marketing Research*, about 7,000 NYT articles | High-arousal emotion (awe, anger, anxiety) drives sharing. Low-arousal sadness suppresses it. | Articles, not video. Seen here via secondary citation. |
| Direct, secondary | Adam Mosseri via [Buffer, Nov 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/) | Top Reels signals: watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach. Sends matter more for non-followers. | Reported by Buffer, not read from Instagram directly. |
| Medium | [Mishra, preprint, 46,605 TikTok hooks](https://sciety.org/articles/activity/10.31235/osf.io/rj2mz_v1) | Curiosity and urgency beat direct address and instructions. Emotional hooks are rare (about 3.2%) but earn more comments and shares. Best length varies by niche, about 9 words in news up to 90 in entertainment. | Not peer reviewed. Measures engagement, not retention. Only the abstract was readable. |
| Humbling | [Paekivi and Karjus, arXiv 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16053), about 10,000 TikToks, 77 structural variables | Most structural variables had no link to success, and prediction gains over account size and age were modest. | Estonian brand accounts. |
| Weak | [Green Frog Labs](https://greenfroglabs.com/blog/anatomy-of-viral-hook), [Same Yeam](https://www.sameyeam.info/viral-short-form-video-formula/), [Hollyland](https://store.hollyland.com/blogs/creator-hub/tiktok-video-script-writing-tips), [Opus](https://www.opus.pro/blog/instagram-reels-hook-formulas) | Practical craft: layers of a hook, open loops, single CTA, 125 to 150 spoken words per minute. | Self-reported or vendor content. Used for craft, never for numbers. |

**Deliberately not used:** the widely repeated "71% decide in 3 seconds", "pattern interrupts raise watch time 85%", and similar figures. They trace back to vendors or to sources that could not be checked.

**The honest bottom line:** nothing guarantees a viral hook. The Paekivi result says formula features explain less than creators assume. These rules raise your floor. Your own retention graphs tell you what works for your audience, so film two hooks for the same script and compare.

## Hooks

1. **Bounded curiosity is the core rule.** Name the concrete subject and the stakes, and withhold the answer. Too vague loses, and telling the whole story loses more often. This is the best-evidenced finding here.
2. **Instantly clear to a stranger.** Most views come from people who have never heard of you. No insider references.
3. **Open one question that only the ending closes.** Answer early and you give people permission to leave.
4. **High-arousal feeling.** Surprise, amusement, awe, indignation or worry. Mild interest doesn't travel.
5. **Specific to the topic.** If another topic could be swapped in, it's a template, not a hook.
6. **Payable.** Bait costs you the rest of the watch time.
7. **One breath.** Roughly 6 to 12 words, adjusted to the niche using real openings scraped for your topic.
8. **Three layers written together.** Spoken line, on-screen text for sound-off viewers, and a first frame already in motion.
9. **Generate many, judge separately.** The app writes 10 across different archetypes, rejects rule-breakers in code, scores the rest as a separate step, and offers you the top 3.

## Body

- Prove the hook within about 5 seconds.
- One idea per video.
- New information every 5 to 8 seconds. Each beat pays something and raises the next question.
- Connect beats with "but", "so" and "which means", not "also" and "next".
- Put one turn in the middle, where the obvious answer proves incomplete.
- Save the strongest point for last.
- Write for the ear: short sentences, contractions, no filler.

Evidence for the body is mostly practitioner consensus plus the curiosity research above.

## Ending

- Fully pay off the hook, then stop within a sentence or two. The peak-end rule says people judge an experience by its high point and its end.
- One action, chosen for this video. "Follow for more" is a placeholder.
- Prefer a send trigger that names a specific person, since sends are the signal Instagram says matters most for reaching non-followers.
- Use a loop when the last line naturally flows into the first.

## Length

Scripts target 150 spoken words per minute: 75 words for 30s, about 112 for 45s, 150 for 60s, 225 for 90s.
